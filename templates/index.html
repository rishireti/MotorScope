The overlooked signal

We track many health signals. Movement is often overlooked.

Most of us pay attention to the numbers a clinician asks about. The numbers that describe how we move are rarely on that list — even though they shift slowly, affect everyday life, and are easy to miss day to day.

Know your baseline

Your baseline is the reference that makes a change visible.

A single reading tells you very little. A personal baseline — what your walking normally looks like for you — is what makes a gradual shift noticeable. Over several months, small differences become a trend you can describe.

Example only Your walking speed has stayed within a narrow range across these months, which makes a sustained shift away from it easier to spot than it would be from a single morning walk.
