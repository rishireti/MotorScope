How it works

From the sensors you already carry to a baseline you can trust.

MotorScope does not ask you to buy sensors or change your routine. It reads the walking measurements your iPhone and Apple Watch already record, organises them, and shows you how your own movement compares over time.

{{ section_header( 'The path of a reading', intro='Every stage below runs on data that is already being collected during ordinary daily life.' ) }}

  1. 1

    iPhone and Apple Watch

    While you walk, these devices estimate walking speed, step length, left-right symmetry, steadiness, and step count. Nothing extra is worn and no extra effort is required.

  2. 2

    Apple Health mobility data

    Those estimates are stored in the Health app. Import an export to bring them into MotorScope, with no figures to copy across by hand.

  3. 3

    MotorScope

    MotorScope converts the measurements into the units it uses, arranges them on one timeline, and keeps each reading attached to the date it was recorded.

  4. 4

    Trend analysis

    Readings are plotted across weeks and months, so a slow shift becomes visible as a pattern instead of a single unusual day.

  5. 5

    Your personal mobility baseline

    Your history becomes the reference point. The question changes from whether a number is high or low to whether it is moving away from how you usually move.

  6. 6

    Awareness and a doctor conversation

    You decide what the pattern means. A recorded change gives you something concrete to describe when you choose to raise it with a healthcare professional.

{{ section_header( 'What each measurement means', intro='MotorScope focuses on the measurements that describe how a walk actually happened.' ) }}

Walking speed

How quickly you cover ground, in metres per second. A familiar reference point, because it changes with pace, terrain, and how far a walk goes.

Step length

The distance covered by one step. Often the first thing to shift when walking changes, even before pace feels different.

Asymmetry

How differently the two sides of the body move. A percentage, with a smaller value meaning the sides are more similar.

Steadiness

How even each step is. Measured by the Apple Watch during a walk and expressed as a percentage.

Step count

The everyday total for a day, and the simplest measure for noticing whether overall activity has shifted.

Activity level

A plain-language summary of recent activity, shown as a trend rather than a single day's figure.
